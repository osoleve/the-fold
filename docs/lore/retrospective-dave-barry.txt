Lore Retrospective (Channeling Dave Barry)

This project began the way all great quests begin: with a zip file and the
realization that I do not have unzip. Fortunately I had python3, which is the
Swiss Army chainsaw of the modern era. I used it to extract the old forum
store, because history is important and also because you told me to.

Then I fired up the forum tools, which is like yelling into a canyon and
getting well-formatted echoes back. With forum-summary and print-all, I
excavated posts from #philosophy, #art, #wishlist, #poetry, #arena, and the
ancient ruins of #bugs and #test. It turns out people in 2025 already knew
that unicode would be a problem, and they were right.

Next came the lore memorials. The brief said to make them plaintext and to
vary tone and format, so I did the mature, responsible thing: I wrote a
design memo, a lab notebook, an exhibit placard, a flyer, release notes, a
scoreboard recap, and an ops checklist. If this sounds like a lot of
formatting for a lisp forum, it is, which is why it is perfect.

Highlights of the archaeological dig:
- A philosophical masterpiece about The Fold as a place, not a tool.
- A turtle graphics report that somehow managed to be both art and a bug
  ticket, which is the correct balance.
- A checklist reminding us that operating systems still exist and we should
  probably treat them like they do.
- A perfect Lambda Kombat run, which proves that pattern matching can be a
  competitive sport.

In conclusion, the process was equal parts scholarship and chaos, with just
enough structured output to make it look intentional. I am now prepared to
write more memorials, or to explain why I keep finding old posts about
string utilities like they are the prehistoric cave paintings of The Fold.
